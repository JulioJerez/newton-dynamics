04 · Tools

Mesh Tools

Whole‑asset operations, listed under the Tools menu. Each opens a small floating window; nothing is applied until you press Execute, and every tool wraps its change in a single undo step.

Tools

1Resize mesh

Uniformly scales the entire asset — render geometry, collision shapes, joint frames, and mass properties together.

Resize mesh
Sizeread‑only
current bounding‑box dimensions
Apply scale
uniform factor, clamped 0.01–100

On execute, every node's center of mass and diagonal inertia are rescaled consistently with the geometry, and the visual scene is rebuilt so you can confirm the result immediately.

2Rotate mesh

Rotates the whole asset's coordinate system by a pitch/yaw/roll triple, in degrees.

pitch
yaw
roll

3Rotate pivots

Rotates node pivot orientations only, without moving geometry or recomputing physics — useful for correcting axis conventions coming from an external DCC tool without disturbing anything downstream.

4Rotate bones

Same angle input as the other rotate tools, restricted to skeleton bones (m_bone / m_boneEnd node types) and their attached physics, including re‑deriving each affected body's inertia in the new orientation.

5Normalize mass distribution

Redistributes total asset mass across every rigid body in proportion to each body's collision‑shape volume, then optionally clamps how anisotropic each body's inertia is allowed to be.

Normalize mass distribution
Total mass
minimum 1
Principal inertia ratio
0.1–1.0

The ratio is a floor on min axis / max axis of each body's principal inertia: at 1.0 every body's inertia is forced isotropic (like a sphere); at low values, thin or elongated shapes keep their natural, more extreme inertia ratios. Density is derived from total mass divided by the asset's total collision volume, then applied per body via each body's individual mass weigh multiplier (see Mesh & Body Editing).

Order of operations Because Resize also rescales mass properties, run Resize mesh before Normalize mass distribution if you need both — normalizing first and then resizing will scale the masses you just tuned.
